AEO vs GEO Explained: The Two Halves of AI Search Optimization

Two acronyms, one goal: getting your business into AI answers. Here's what each actually does — and why you can't skip either.

If you've started researching AI search optimization, you've hit two terms: AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) and GEO (Generative Engine Optimization). They're often used interchangeably, but they're two distinct halves of the same job.

What AEO actually is

AEO is about making your content answer questions directly. The goal: when an AI engine looks for the answer to a query, it finds yours — structured so it can be quoted.

This is content work:

  • Pages built around real questions buyers ask
  • Direct answers in the first paragraphs
  • FAQ schema and structured data so the model can parse it
  • Complete coverage of subtopics, comparisons, and objections

Think of AEO as making your website speak the AI's language.

What GEO actually is

GEO is about where your brand appears in generated answers — and that goes far beyond your own site. AI engines assemble answers from many sources: directories, review platforms, industry publications, forums, and news sites. GEO is engineering your presence across all of them.

This is authority and placement work:

  • Listings on the platforms AI engines already cite in your niche
  • Mentions on high-authority sites and communities
  • Consistent entity signals across the whole web
  • Reviews and reputation signals that engines weigh

Think of GEO as being in the places the AI reads.

The difference in one line

AEO gets your content picked; GEO gets your brand picked. AEO makes you quotable. GEO makes you present. Both are required — a perfectly optimized website no AI engine reads gets cited as often as a well-known brand with no answer-ready content (that is: almost never).

Why you need both

Data from AI visibility studies shows the pattern clearly: brands that lead a topic in AI answers usually have both strong on-site answer content and off-site authority presence. The runner-up with a stronger domain but weaker topical focus still loses.

In practice:

  • Skip AEO → your content never gets quoted, even if your brand is known
  • Skip GEO → AI engines can't verify you're established, so they recommend competitors instead

Where to start

  1. Measure both halves. Audit where you appear (GEO) and whether your content answers the queries (AEO).
  2. Fix the entity foundation. Consistent name, category, location everywhere.
  3. Build answer content. Question-matched articles with FAQ schema.
  4. Earn placements. Get onto the authority sites in your niche.
  5. Monitor and repeat. AI visibility shifts weekly; keep closing gaps.
